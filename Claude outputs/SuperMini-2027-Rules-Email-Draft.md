**Subject: Proposed 2027 technical regulations**

Hi all,

Before we submit our category regulations to Motorsport Australia for 2027, the committee is putting the following rule changes to members for a vote in principle at the October members' meeting.

**Why**

The longevity of the category and parity between the cars. A category lasts when people can afford to stay in it and believe they can win in it. Right now the engine is where the money goes, the gap between a built car and a stock one is measured in seconds, and reliability decided rounds this year.

No ruleset is perfect and rules will always be tested. This revision is intended to make the things worth exploiting small, so the next dollar spent buys tenths rather than seconds, and to bring the cars close enough that the driver decides the result. The approach is to control the air the engine can use and the tune that runs it, rather than the parts inside it, so the parts already on the cars stay on the cars. Nothing comes off any car except the supercharger pulley.

The figures below are estimates. Before submission the package will be validated on a dyno with cars from our field, and the control tune built and tested on both a stock head and the best head in the class.

**Engine**

Control camshaft. One nominated profile, run as supplied on standard cam wheels. No adjustable or offset wheels. The cam most of the field already runs will be nominated.
Why: a free cam is the cheapest top-end power and the hardest to police. One profile removes the variable and makes a single tune possible.

Stock supercharger pulley. Standard supercharger and crank pulleys. The 17% overdrive and 4% balancer allowance are removed.
Why: at 7,000 rpm the 17% pulley spins the blower to about 17,900 rpm, past the speed the pulley makers design to. It is the main cause of high intake temperatures, timing being pulled in traffic, and thrown belts. The stock pulley drops intake temperature 12–18° at the sensor and restores belt wrap and tensioner travel. It costs about 20 hp for every car equally.

Inlet restrictor. A control plate with a fixed-diameter orifice between the throttle body and the supercharger. All air entering the engine passes through it. Sized so a standard-head car reaches choke at 7,000 rpm.
Why: it caps how much air any engine can use at the top of the rev range, so at the end of the straight every car is on the same air regardless of the head. A ported head still has an advantage below the choke, on corner exit, but a much smaller one than today: roughly two to three tenths a lap instead of the best part of a second. The restrictor does not make heads equal; it makes the difference small enough that the head stops deciding races.

Cylinder head free.
Why: a stock-head rule would require every car with a ported head to fit a standard one, and a ported head cannot be returned to stock. Standard heads are not equal either: castings vary, and the advantage would go to whoever flow-tests the most heads, which cannot be policed. A port cannot be measured in the pit lane. With the restrictor, the best option is a Lohen head off the shelf at a known price. A competitor may port their own head, but it will not outperform the catalogue part by a meaningful margin.

Compression 8.5:1 maximum, measured at Tier 3.
Why: compression is the one bottom-end change that still pays under a restrictor, and the cap keeps every engine inside the tune's knock margin.

Control exhaust manifold. One nominated manifold for every car, fitted unmodified.
Why: the restrictor does not reach the exhaust side, and a header is worth a few percent in the midrange. One part removes the variable and is checked visually.

Locked control tune. One calibration on the Link G4X, loaded and verified by the eligibility officer. Closed-loop on lambda with knock control. Competitor-adjustable: idle, cold start, fan, gauges only.
Why: the tune is the largest single variable under the current rules; identical engines with two tuners can be 5–8% apart. One calibration removes it, makes every car's data comparable, and is safer than relying on each tuner's judgement.

Fuel. 98 octane fuel only. Sampling fitting on every car; a sample may be taken at any time. Nominated injector.
Why: the locked tune is built for one fuel and one injector. Ethanol blends inside the trim range would give a knock-limited engine more timing on hot laps, so fuel has to be checkable.

**Weight**

Minimum racing weight 1,200 kg with driver, down from 1,225 kg.
Why: the stock pulley takes about 20 hp from every car. Lowering the minimum weight by 25 kg recovers some of that lap time at no cost, and most cars can reach it by removing ballast.

**Suspension**

Dampers remain free. A control damper package is being discussed. If adopted, a grandfather period of at least 24 months will apply so that existing cars can continue to use their current equipment.
Why: dampers are the largest equipment gap left, but a control damper also writes off the most existing money. A long notice period lets that equipment run its useful life and stops anyone buying the wrong thing in the meantime.

Setup limits: maximum 4.0° negative camber; a minimum ride height with driver aboard; elastomeric bushes with no metallic elements; anti-roll bars solid and non-adjustable, with maximum diameters; links without spherical bearings. All pit-lane checks. The ride height minimum and bar diameters will be set from measurement of current cars at Morgan Park so nobody is made illegal by the numbers.
Why: the same limits the Excels run. It closes the setup arms race without changing a part on any car.

**Tyres**

Four new tyres per round, registered at scrutineering.
Why: fresh rubber against a two-round-old set is comparable to any engine difference in the class, and it is the most visible running cost.

**Classes**

One class: the R50/R53 Cooper and Cooper S specification. Unlimited cars are Group 3D Sport Sedans, invited under the supplementary regulations and classified separately.
Why: the regulations are submitted as a single category so that, if Cooper Class consistently fields more than 15 cars every weekend, Cooper can have its own grid and Unlimited can run with Sports Sedans on the same race weekend. We would ensure both remain on the same weekends so the club stays together as it is now. A single specification is also simpler to write, police and submit.

**Scrutineering**

No sealing. Inspection is a standing right of the eligibility officer.
Why: under these rules almost everything that matters is a pit-lane measurement.

Tier 1, pit lane, every round: weight, camber, ride height, track, tyres, restrictor, pulley diameters, manifold, calibration file, sensors, fuel sample, camera. Random draw plus the round winner.
Tier 2, cam cover off: cam and timing. Any top-five championship car after the last race of a meeting, at the eligibility officer's discretion.
Tier 3, head off: compression ratio. The championship winner's car within 14 days of the final round, category-funded. The championship is not declared until it clears.
Why: the cars with the most to gain are inspected most closely, everyone knows it in advance, and the category funds the one inspection that costs real money.

ECU logs may be collected from any car and compared across the field.
Why: with every car on the same tune, the logs are a like-for-like record and show whether the restrictor size is right without lifting a head.

The pole-sitter's ECU log and onboard video from qualifying are made available to every competitor after the meeting.
Why: the fastest car's data and setup are visible to the whole field.

Non-compliance forfeits the meeting's points, or the season's if found at the season inspection. Protest fees scale with the tier and costs follow the result. No parity, handicap or success ballast.
Why: penalties are predictable, and the cost of a protest is borne by the party found to be wrong. Performance is equalised by controlling the car, not adjusting the result.

**Power: before and after**

Estimated crank horsepower at QR, same driver, same tyres. "Midrange" is 5,000–6,000 rpm, the corner-exit band.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stock engine, control cam, 8.5:1 | $3,000 | ~200 hp | — | ~180 hp | — |
| Plus Lohen or equivalent head | $10,000 | ~220 hp | +20 hp, ~0.8 s/lap | ~184 hp | +4 hp peak, +7–10 hp midrange, ~0.2–0.3 s/lap |
| Full build: head, cam, tune, forged bottom end | $21,000 | ~245 hp | +45 hp, ~1.5–2 s/lap | ~187 hp | +7 hp peak, +7–10 hp midrange, ~0.3 s/lap |

Every car loses about 20 hp to the stock pulley. Every car is on the same air at the end of the straight. What money still buys is 7–10 hp on corner exit, worth two to three tenths, at a cost of about $7,000.

**Cost to comply**

17% pulley: standard pulley and belt, $150–300.
Cam other than the nominated one: control cam, $1,000–1,300.
Any head: nothing.
Manifold other than the nominated one: control manifold, $1,000–1,500.
Any Link tune: control tune loaded at scrutineering, no cost.
Dampers: nothing.

Every car currently racing is legal on day one apart from the pulley, and the cam and manifold if they differ. No engine rebuilds are required.

**Before submission**

A dyno day with three cars (stock head, ported head, best head in the field) on the control cam and stock pulley, to size the restrictor and build the tune.

Measurement of anti-roll bar diameters and ride height on current cars, and throttle body bore, so the figures come from Minis rather than the Excel regulations.

A Technical Guide covering the recommended engine build at each budget, the catalogue heads known to work, and the cooling and front-drive checklists.

Survey responses on diffs and dampers, to inform the control damper discussion and whether a control diff is nominated alongside it.

**Timeline**

October: members' meeting, vote in principle.
November: dyno day, regulations finalised.
December: submitted to Motorsport Australia and published.
Commencement date: regulations take effect.
Control dampers, if adopted: not less than 24 months after nomination.

**Unchanged**

The eligible car, block and bottom end, intercooler, cooling, exhaust behind the manifold, gearbox and diff, brakes, body and cockpit, control tyre, 7,000 rpm rev limit.
