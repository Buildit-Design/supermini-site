Gilchrist and Mills crowned 2026 Australian Champions

Decided at the last corner of the last lap

The 2026 Rubber Craft Australian Championship came to a close at The Bend Motorsport Park on 21–23 August, and by Sunday evening the SuperMini Challenge had two new champions. Brock Gilchrist takes the Cooper Class title, and Andrew Mills the Unlimited Class. Two drivers who did their season's work the hard way, and two thoroughly deserved titles.

Gilchrist finishes on 178 points, twelve clear of Josh Mills on 166, a Cooper Class title that came down to a last-lap pass in the final race of the season. In the Unlimited Class, Andrew Mills closes on 180 from Brayden Larkin on 107 and Gregg Chesterfield on 106.

Cooper: settled on the last lap

Josh Mills arrived in South Australia twenty points adrift and set about erasing it. He won the first three Cooper races of the weekend and took the class fastest lap in every one of them, and by the time the cars rolled out for the final race the gap was down to eight points.

That last race is the one they'll be talking about. Mills led, Gilchrist shadowed him, and on the final lap Gilchrist went down the inside into turn five and took the lead. Mills fought all the way to the flag and couldn't find a way back through. Gilchrist won it by 0.363 seconds, and took the fastest lap with it.

Gilchrist had spent the whole weekend on the back foot and never let it show. The margins in the earlier races had been seven and a half seconds, seven and a half again, and eight tenths, and through all of it he kept his head, kept the car in the points he needed, and waited. Then, with a championship on the line and a rival in the form of his life ahead of him, he produced the drive of the weekend. Staying with a quicker car for three straight races and then passing it on the final lap is about as good an answer as a champion can give.

On the arithmetic he needed only a third place to be certain of it. He went and won it instead, which tells you most of what you need to know about how Brock races. Josh Mills closed the deficit from twenty points to twelve after a superb weekend of his own, and finishes the year runner-up on 166 with Trent Spencer third on 134.

Unlimited: Andrew Mills closes it out

Andrew Mills arrived in South Australia with the championship already built, and drove exactly the weekend the situation asked for. Second, third, third and third, with no mistakes, no risks that weren't worth taking, and a finish every time the flag fell. Titles are won that way far more often than they are won with headline results, and 180 points to Brayden Larkin's 107 and Gregg Chesterfield's 106 is the measure of how well he judged the season from Morgan Park onwards.

There was no shortage of speed around him either. Craig Lindsell won three of the four Open races and lifted himself from 22 points to 100 across a single weekend, finishing the championship fifth. James Campbell set the fastest lap of the weekend at 2:06.0769, claimed fastest lap in three of the four races, and won the finale, ending the year fourth on 104. Banking the results he needed in that company is no small thing.

An interstate weekend

The round ran as part of an eighteen-car combined Mini field, with eight SuperMini Challenge entries racing alongside South Australian and Victorian competitors. It is a reminder of how far the category now reaches beyond its Queensland home.

Queensland is still live

The Australian Championship is done, but the season is not. Two rounds of the Rubber Craft Queensland State Championship remain: Queensland Raceway on 19–20 September, then Morgan Park on 6–8 November.

Gilchrist leads the Queensland Cooper Class on 293 from Josh Mills on 240. The Unlimited Class is tighter still. Gregg Chesterfield heads the table on 265, with Anthony Elliott and Brayden Larkin locked together on 190 and everything to sort out between them.

Congratulations to Brock and to Andrew. Two champions who earned it in very different ways, and both of them the right way. Thanks as well to everyone who made the trip to The Bend, and to the South Australian and Victorian crews who made it a weekend worth winning.
