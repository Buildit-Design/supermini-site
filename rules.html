Rules & Regs

The regulations that keep SuperMini Challenge racing close, fair and safe. This page summarises the key points — the published appendix is the authority.

Download 2026 regulations (PDF)

2026 Racing Appendix · 3rd Category, Group 3X · last updated 15 November 2025 · Motorsport Australia

Overview

Run by the book

SuperMini Challenge Inc is a Motorsport Australia–affiliated, Queensland-incorporated non-profit club that began in 2022. Every round is run under the Motorsport Australia National Competition Rules and the RACERS guidelines, alongside the SMC's own sporting and technical regulations for the Cooper and Unlimited classes.

The rules are written for club-level racing for the purpose of fun — a balanced, equitable series for the drivers and cars competing in it. Where the rules don't say you can do something, you can't, and the Management Committee has final say.

Technical

The two classes

All competitors race in one of two classes. Control RubberCraft Racing tyres are mandatory in both.

Cooper Class

The entry-level class, built around the BMW R50 & R53 Mini Cooper / Cooper S (2001–2006). Cars stay largely OEM, with a defined list of permitted modifications.

  • Control LINK G4X Plugin ECU (SKU 209-4000), firmware set by the category manager each event
  • 7,000 rpm limit, with a handicap RPM system run by the category manager to keep racing close
  • Pump fuel compliant with Schedule G of the Motorsport Australia Manual
  • Minimum racing weight 1,225 kg with driver
  • Maximum wheel 17" × 7.5"; maximum track 1,750 mm
  • Control tyre: RubberCraft SP-01R 225/45-R17

Unlimited Class

The Unlimited Class, for faster and more developed Minis — run to Motorsport Australia Group 3D Sport Sedan regulations, subject to the amendments in the SMC appendix.

  • Any two-wheel-drive BMW Mini is eligible, but must remain front-wheel drive — 4WD is not permitted
  • Cars must remain floorpan cars as defined in Group 3D
  • Engine stays in the original bay, set back no further than the firewall
  • No minimum-weight rule; aftermarket internals and gearbox free, ABS free
  • Maximum wheel 17" × 9"; bodywork may extend up to 250 mm forward of the factory silhouette
  • Control tyres: RubberCraft RC001 (220/620-R17 & 240/610-R17)

Full technical detail — engine, transmission, suspension, brakes, electrical, bodywork and weight — is in sections 6–18 of the 2026 appendix, with component specifications and diagrams in appendices 1–10.

Mandatory equipment

Safety & presentation

In-car camera

A forward-facing video camera recording to SD card is mandatory in every car. Footage must be supplied on request — no video means no protest rights.

Safety cage & rain light

Every car must have a safety cage compliant with Schedule J of the Motorsport Australia Manual, and a rain light centrally mounted on the rear hatch above the number-plate recess.

Livery

The SMC windscreen banner and current sponsor decals are compulsory on every car, even those with full livery. A 150 × 150 mm class letter — U or C — goes below the race number on the windscreen.

On track

Driving standards & penalties

SMC races under the RACERS driving guidelines, with a Driving Standards Officer (DSO) appointed for each event. The aim is close racing with absolutely minimal contact — no driver may gain an advantage through car-to-car contact, and any unfair advantage must be handed back within one lap.

Not tolerated: blocking or weaving, crowding an overtaking car, late dives that cause contact, "bump and run," and running beyond the white track-edge lines.

Typical penalties: 30 seconds added to race time or a pit-lane drive-through; a 10-place grid penalty for code-of-conduct breaches in qualifying or for frivolous protests; and exclusion (up to three further events) for careless or dangerous driving. One warning is allowed per competitor per meeting. Rolling starts at approximately 80 km/h are the standard format.

A further point goes to the driver setting the fastest lap in each class in each race, provided they are classified as a finisher. There are no points for pole position. Round winners are declared in each class at every round.

Class champions are crowned in both Cooper and Unlimited. No points are awarded for a DNF — you must complete 75% of race distance and take the chequered flag. Ties are split by number of wins, then seconds, then thirds, then qualifying.

Championship structure, membership and driving standards sit in the club's sporting regulations, which are issued separately from the technical appendix above. Contact us for the current version.

Parity

Keeping it close

To keep Cooper Class racing tight, the category manager runs a handicap system against the 7,000 rpm ceiling, with added weight carried in the passenger footwell where required — aiming to keep the top five within about one second per lap. To reduce "breakouts," lap timers and predictive timing are forbidden during racing, though basic timers may be permitted in qualifying with approval.
